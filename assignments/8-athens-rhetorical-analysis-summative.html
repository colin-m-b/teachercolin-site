Rhetorical Analysis Essay

8 Athens · MYP 3 Language and Literature · Unit 1 Summative Assessment · Criteria A, B, C, D

Essay question

How does the speaker move the audience through a series of emotions to reach a persuasive conclusion, and how does the speaker create those emotions?

Write a rhetorical analysis essay of 600–800 words on the speech you annotated and outlined.

The three-part process

  1. Annotate (formative). Annotate a printed copy of your chosen speech, tracking the emotions the speaker creates and the techniques that create them.
  2. Outline (formative). Plan your essay in note form: evidence, introduction and thesis, at least three PEEL body paragraphs, and a conclusion. You will use this outline when you write.
  3. Write (summative). Write your essay by hand in class over two 80-minute sessions.

Speech choices

  • Malala Yousafzai, speech to the United Nations Youth Assembly (2013)
  • Mary Fisher, "A Whisper of AIDS" (1992)
  • Barack Obama, Sandy Hook interfaith prayer vigil speech (2012)

Your essay must include

  • An introduction that gives the context (speaker, occasion, audience) and ends with your thesis.
  • At least three body paragraphs following PEEL, one for each stage of the emotional journey, in the order the stages appear in the speech.
  • A conclusion that restates your thesis in new words and explains why the emotional journey persuades.

Conditions

  • Two 80-minute sessions, in class.
  • Handwritten in blue or black pen, on the writing paper provided. Number every page.
  • No devices.
  • Length: 600–800 words. Write your word count on the cover page when you finish.
  • All papers are collected at the end of Session 1 and returned at the start of Session 2. Nothing goes home.

On your desk

Allowed

  • Your annotated speech (Part 1)
  • Your outline (Part 2), in note form
  • Your extra paragraphs sheet, if used
  • Pens and a highlighter

Not allowed

  • Devices of any kind
  • Pre-written paragraphs or full drafts
  • Any notes other than your annotations and outline

Remember

  • Use academic writing conventions: formal diction, no hedging, an objective voice, precise word choice, no idioms or slang.
  • Embed short quotations in your own sentences and give the ¶ number, for example: (¶ 4).
  • Explain HOW the speaker's words create each emotion and WHY that moves the audience. Do not retell the speech.
  • Save time at the end of Session 2 to proofread.

Tips for parents

  • The essay is written by hand in class and nothing goes home, so the best help is conversation, not drafting. Please do not write or edit the essay for your child.
  • Ask your child to explain the essay question in their own words: how does a speaker move an audience from one emotion to the next?
  • Ask your child to talk you through their annotated speech. Which emotions did they find, and which words create them?
  • Ask your child to explain their outline out loud, one PEEL paragraph at a time. Explaining it is good practice for writing it.
  • Make sure your child has a blue or black pen and a highlighter for both sessions.

Rubric

Each criterion is marked out of 8 (total 32). Start with the lowest band and move up, and choose the band that best fits the essay as a whole.

Criterion A: Analyzing (8 Athens)

Strands: (i) identify and explain the content, context, language, structure, technique, and style of a text; (ii) identify and explain the effects of the creator's choices on an audience; (iii) justify opinions and ideas using examples, explanations, and terminology. Strand (iv), interpreting similarities and differences between texts, is not assessed.

BandDescriptor
0The essay does not reach a standard described by any of the descriptors below.
1–2Limited. The essay mostly retells the speech. A few emotions or techniques are named, with little explanation of their effect on the audience. Evidence is missing or not explained. Little terminology.
3–4Adequate. The essay identifies the main emotions and some techniques and explains their effects in general terms. Some explanation retells the evidence. Some terminology is used.
5–6Substantial. The essay explains how specific words and techniques create each emotion and how the emotions move the audience toward the speaker's purpose. Evidence supports each point. Terminology is used accurately.
7–8Excellent. The essay explains in detail how the speaker builds an emotional journey, showing how each stage leads to the next and to the persuasive conclusion. Evidence is well chosen and terminology is used accurately throughout.

Criterion B: Organizing (8 Athens)

Strands: (i) employ organizational structures that serve the context and intention; (ii) organize opinions and ideas in a coherent and logical manner; (iii) use referencing and formatting tools.

BandDescriptor
0The essay does not reach a standard described by any of the descriptors below.
1–2Minimal. The introduction, thesis, or conclusion is missing. Paragraphs do not follow PEEL and ideas are hard to follow. Quotations are rarely referenced.
3–4Adequate. The essay has an introduction, body paragraphs, and a conclusion, but PEEL is used inconsistently. The order of ideas is sometimes unclear. Some quotations are referenced.
5–6Competent. At least three PEEL paragraphs follow the order of the emotional journey. The thesis guides the essay, and transitions connect paragraphs. Quotations are referenced with ¶ numbers.
7–8Sophisticated. The essay is clearly and logically organized from start to finish. Transitions show how each emotional stage leads to the next. Quotations are embedded and consistently referenced.

Criterion C: Producing text (8 Athens)

Strands: (i) demonstrate insight and personal engagement with ideas; (ii) make stylistic choices with awareness of the impact on an audience; (iii) select relevant details and examples to develop ideas.

BandDescriptor
0The essay does not reach a standard described by any of the descriptors below.
1–2Limited. Ideas are obvious or undeveloped. Few stylistic choices suit an academic reader. Details and examples are few or not relevant.
3–4Some. Ideas are developed in places but stay general. Some stylistic choices suit an academic reader. Details and examples are mostly relevant.
5–6Considerable. Ideas are developed thoughtfully, with some original thinking about how the speech works. Stylistic choices suit an academic reader. Relevant details develop each idea.
7–8High degree. The essay shows original thinking about why the emotional journey persuades. Stylistic choices are deliberate and effective. Details are well chosen and fully developed.

Criterion D: Using language (8 Athens)

Strands: (i) use appropriate and varied vocabulary, sentence structures, and forms of expression; (ii) write in a register and style that serve the context and intention; (iii) use correct grammar, syntax, and punctuation; (iv) spell with accuracy. Strand (v), non-verbal communication, is not assessed.

BandDescriptor
0The essay does not reach a standard described by any of the descriptors below.
1–2Limited. Vocabulary and sentence structures are limited. The register is often informal (slang, hedging, personal opinion). Frequent errors in grammar, punctuation, and spelling make meaning unclear.
3–4Adequate. Vocabulary and sentence structures show some variety. The register is mostly formal, with some lapses. Errors are noticeable but meaning is generally clear.
5–6Varied. Vocabulary and sentence structures are varied. A formal academic register is used consistently. Errors are few and do not affect meaning.
7–8Effective. Vocabulary is precise and sentence structures are varied for effect. The academic register is consistent and effective. Grammar, punctuation, and spelling are highly accurate.
