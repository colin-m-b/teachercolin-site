Unit 2 — Protest Songs and Poetry

We study how songwriters and poets use creative self-expression to speak out against injustice, and how they shape their words, sound, and performance to inspire audiences to join the fight for a fairer world.

Purpose for the unit

Throughout history, ordinary people have used songs and poems to stand up to injustice, from the Civil Rights movement to workers' strikes to anti-war protests. In this unit we study how writers like Maya Angelou, Langston Hughes, and Wilfred Owen use rhythm, repetition, imagery, and tone to move an audience to act. Learning how this works shows students that creativity is a real form of power, not just decoration. It also prepares them to recognize when words are being used to inspire them, and to use their own voice for the causes they care about.

Key concept: Creativity · Global context: Fairness and development
