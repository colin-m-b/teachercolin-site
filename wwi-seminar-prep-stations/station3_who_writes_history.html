Who Writes History?

Is history written by the winners? How much does a historian’s own time and place shape their view?

This station prepares you for

Q2. Is what we inherently believe about history true or is it fabricated by historians to support a narrative that demonizes the loser in battle?

Q6. To what extent does the context of the time they live in, their present, shape historians’ perspectives about things that happened in the past?

The claim you are testing
“History is written by the winners to make the loser look bad.”
Who won? In 1918, the winning side included Britain, France, the USA and Australia (as part of the British Empire). The losing side included Germany and Austria-Hungary.

The historians

Read each historian’s context and verdict. Then complete the table on your worksheet. Decide for yourself whether each historian supports or challenges the claim.
1928Sidney B. Fay
Who and whenAmerican, 1928
ContextWrote soon after the Treaty of Versailles blamed Germany. Governments, led by Germany, were publishing their pre-war diplomatic documents.
VerdictGerman leaders were “simpletons” who handed the rope to “a stupid and clumsy adventurer” (Berchtold, Austria-Hungary). They were “not criminals plotting the World War,” but carried “a grave responsibility.”
1960Gerhard Ritter
Who and whenWest German, born 1888; this volume 1960
ContextA conservative historian who fought in the First World War himself.
Verdict“No one in a leading position wished to bring about a world war.” Political blindness was “by no means limited to Vienna and Berlin.”
1961Fritz Fischer
Who and whenWest German, 1961
ContextUsed German government archives that only became available after 1945. Wrote while West Germany was confronting its Nazi past.
VerdictGermany “willed and coveted the Austro-Serbian war” and its leaders “must bear a substantial share of the historical responsibility.”
2012Christopher Clark
Who and whenAustralian, working at Cambridge, 2012
ContextWrote for the war’s 100th anniversary. Uses Serbian and Russian sources more than earlier Western historians.
Verdict“There is no smoking gun in this story; or, rather, there is one in the hands of every major character.” The war was “a tragedy, not a crime.”
2013Max Hastings
Who and whenBritish journalist and military historian, 2013
ContextAlso wrote for the 100th anniversary, a year after Clark.
Verdict“The most important immediate cause of the First World War was that Germany chose to support an Austrian invasion of Serbia.” Blame “must rest in Vienna.”
