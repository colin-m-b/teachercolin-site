Fact or Interpretation?

What is the difference between a fact about the past and an interpretation of it?

This station prepares you for

Q1. Why do historians need to evaluate and interpret an event that happened long ago rather than coming to a conclusive timeline and verdict based on known facts?

Q7. What if everybody agrees on one interpretation of an event? Does that mean that this interpretation should be taken as correct? Why or why not?

Concept to use: power. Who had the power to write the first verdict on the war, and whose voice was missing?

Part B: Did everyone ever agree?

Read the five moments below, in order. Then answer the Part B questions on your worksheet.
1919
Treaty of Versailles
The peace treaty blamed Germany for the war. It was written by the countries that won.
1928
Sidney B. Fay (American)
German leaders were “not criminals plotting the World War” but carried “a grave responsibility for what happened later.”
1960
Gerhard Ritter (West German)
“No one in a leading position wished to bring about a world war; and to that extent there is no longer today any question of ‘war guilt.’”
1961
Fritz Fischer (West German)
Germany’s leaders “must bear a substantial share of the historical responsibility for the outbreak of general war in 1914.”
2012
Christopher Clark (Australian)
“There is no smoking gun in this story; or, rather, there is one in the hands of every major character.”
