Context Upgrade

Don’t just mention the history. Use it to explain the song!
Station 512 minutes
Level 3–4 (Adequate)

“Mentions context but doesn’t link it to meaning.”

Level 5–6 (Competent)

“Uses context to explain the song’s message.”

From the Criterion A rubric, strand i: how well you use context.

Draft 1: a song we studied in class
XSentence X

“Swing Low, Sweet Chariot” is a spiritual that was sung by enslaved people in America.

YSentence Y

Because enslaved people could be punished for talking openly about freedom, the image of a “sweet chariot” coming to carry the singer “home” could hold a hidden meaning: escape to freedom, not only a journey to heaven.

Draft 2: a labor song we studied in class
PSentence P

Because many coal miners were paid in “scrip” that could only be spent at the company-owned store, the line “I owe my soul to the company store” shows how debt trapped the miners and made the company feel like their owner.

QSentence Q

“Sixteen Tons” is a song about coal miners that was written by Merle Travis in 1946.

Your turn: a context sentence that works
Because [a specific fact about the time, place or event], the [lyric or musical choice] [creates this meaning or effect].
  • Names one specific fact, such as a date, event, law or place
  • Links it with because, so, or which means
  • Explains a lyric or a musical choice, not just the song in general

Your task (write in your packet)

  1. For each draft, decide which sentence is Level 3–4 and which is Level 5–6.
  2. Underline the context fact in the stronger sentence. Then explain what the stronger sentence does with that fact.
  3. Choose another song we studied in class: a spiritual, or a labor song such as “9 to 5” or “Take This Job and Shove It.” Write your own Level 5–6 context sentence about it, using a fact about when and why it was sung. Check it against the checklist.
