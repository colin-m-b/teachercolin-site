A sample of the laws of the

Jim Crow South

From the 1880s into the 1960s, laws like these decided where Black Americans could sit, eat, learn, work, play, marry, and be buried.

About the language: these laws use the words “colored” and “negro.” Those were legal terms at the time and are offensive today. When you write, use “Black” or “African American.”
ABusesAlabama

All passenger stations in this state operated by any motor transportation company shall have separate waiting rooms…

BRestaurantsGeorgia

All persons licensed to conduct a restaurant, shall serve either white people exclusively…

CBarbersGeorgia

No colored barber shall serve as a barber [to] white women or girls.

DEducationFlorida

The schools for white children and the schools for negro children shall be conducted separately.

ETextbooksNorth Carolina

Books shall not be interchangeable between the white and colored schools…

FLibrariesNorth Carolina

The state librarian is directed to fit up and maintain a separate place for the use of the colored people…

GParksGeorgia

It shall be unlawful for colored people to frequent any park owned or maintained by the city for the benefit, use and enjoyment of white persons…and unlawful for any white person to frequent any park owned or maintained by the city for the use and benefit of colored persons.

HMiningOklahoma

The baths and lockers for the negroes shall be separate from the white race, but may be in the same building.

IMarriageGeorgia

It shall be unlawful for a white person to marry anyone except a white person.

JBurialGeorgia

The officer in charge shall not bury, or allow to be buried, any colored persons upon ground set apart…

“…” means the law continues. Only the first part is printed here.

Your task (packet page 1)

  1. Sort. Write each law’s letter in the right row of the “Areas of life” table.
  2. React. Which law surprised you most? Explain why in one or two sentences.
  3. Imagine. It is 1955. You are a Black teenager in a Southern town. Using at least three laws (give their letters), describe what an ordinary day would be like.
  4. Predict. Look at the song list on your packet. Which song do you think responds most directly to one of these laws? Explain.
