FIELD RECORDING SESSION · TWO TAPES

Listen Like an Analyst

Station 412 minutes

Your task

Freedom songs work through sound as much as words. At this station you listen to two real recordings and describe the music, not just the lyrics. Use headphones, or share one speaker quietly.

  1. Play Tape 1 all the way through. Fill in the Tape 1 column of the listening log on your packet.
  2. Play Tape 2 all the way through. Fill in the Tape 2 column.
  3. Use the musical terms below. Then answer the two “effect” questions on your packet.
TAPE 1recorded 1963 · about 3 min

This Little Light of Mine

Fannie Lou Hamer

Hamer was a sharecropper and voting-rights organizer from Mississippi. This was one of her favorite songs, and she often sang it at meetings.

Video not playing? Open it on YouTube.
TAPE 2about 2½ min

Ain’t Gonna Let Nobody Turn Me Round

The SNCC Freedom Singers

The Freedom Singers were young activists who formed a group in 1962 in Albany, Georgia, and toured the country singing to spread news of the movement. This song is based on an older spiritual.

Video not playing? Open it on YouTube.

Musical terms to use

solo / groupOne singer, or many singers together.
call and responseA leader sings a line and the group answers.
harmonyDifferent notes sung at the same time, blending together.
a cappellaSinging with no instruments.
tempoThe speed of the music: slow, steady, fast.
rhythm / beatThe pulse you could clap or march to.
dynamicsHow loud or soft the music is, and how that changes.
vocal deliveryHow the voice sounds: strained, shouted, gentle, joyful, rough.
repetitionWords or lines that come back again and again.
lyric substitutionSwapping new words into a familiar song.
