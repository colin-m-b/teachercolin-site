Order of the mass meeting

Why Sing?

A freedom singer explains what the songs did for the movement.

Station 312 minutes
MASS MEETINGSMARCHESJAIL CELLS

Your task (write in your packet)

  1. Bernice Johnson Reagon was a teenager in Albany, Georgia, when she joined the movement. She was jailed for protesting and became a song leader with the SNCC Freedom Singers. Later she wrote about what the songs did.
  2. Each person in your group picks a different reading from her essay below. (In a group of four, two people can share one.)
  3. Open the essay, scroll to your heading, and read only the paragraphs listed. Answer your reading’s questions on your packet.
  4. In the last 3 minutes, take turns explaining your reading to the group in under one minute. Then answer the “Together” question.

Open the essay on PBS →

Choose one reading

Reading A
How to Think of Freedom Songs
Read: paragraphs 3 and 4 under this heading. They begin “What is interesting about…” and “If you listen to recordings…”
Focus: how the songs were sung, and who sang them.
Your questions
A1. What is the difference between a songleader and a soloist?
A2. Why would this way of singing suit a movement made of ordinary people?
Reading B
The Impact of Freedom Singing
Read: paragraphs 3 and 4 under this heading. They begin “There is a great example…” and “There is a story of a policeman…”
Focus: what happened when activists sang in jail and in the street.
Your questions
B1. How did the jailed Freedom Riders change their songs, and why?
B2. What does Reagon say singing did for the people in the movement?
Reading C
Singing in the Face of Danger
Read: both paragraphs under this heading. They begin “I was in a mass meeting…” and “There was a very strong…”
Focus: fear, and what music could and could not do about it.
Your questions
C1. Why was it dangerous when the sheriff came into the meeting?
C2. Reagon says singing did not make fear disappear. So what did it do?

Together

Each reading shows a different job that freedom songs did. As a group, list the jobs. Then choose one and name a feature of a song (such as repetition, call and response, harmony, or changing the words) that would help a song do that job.

Source
